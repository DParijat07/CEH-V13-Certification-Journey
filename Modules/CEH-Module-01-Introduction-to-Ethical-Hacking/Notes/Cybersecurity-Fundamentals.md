# Cybersecurity Fundamentals

## Overview

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting digital systems, networks, applications, devices, and data from cyber threats, unauthorized access, misuse, disruption, modification, or destruction.

It combines people, processes, and technology to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information while enabling organizations to operate securely in the digital world.

As businesses increasingly rely on cloud computing, mobile devices, IoT, and online services, cybersecurity has become a critical business function rather than just an IT responsibility.

---

# What is Cybersecurity?

## Definition

Cybersecurity is the discipline of protecting:

- Computers
- Networks
- Servers
- Mobile devices
- Applications
- Cloud environments
- Digital information

from cyber attacks and unauthorized access.

---

# Objectives of Cybersecurity

The primary objectives are to:

- Protect sensitive information
- Prevent unauthorized access
- Detect cyber attacks
- Respond to security incidents
- Recover from disruptions
- Maintain business continuity
- Meet legal and regulatory requirements
- Preserve customer trust

---

# Importance of Cybersecurity

Organizations rely on cybersecurity to:

- Protect customer data
- Secure financial transactions
- Safeguard intellectual property
- Prevent financial losses
- Maintain operational availability
- Comply with regulations
- Protect organizational reputation

Without effective cybersecurity, organizations face increased risks of data breaches, ransomware attacks, service disruptions, and regulatory penalties.

# Information Security vs Cybersecurity

## Information Security (InfoSec)

Information Security protects information in any form, including:

- Digital files
- Paper documents
- Emails
- Audio recordings
- Printed reports
- Databases

Its goal is to ensure confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information regardless of where it is stored.

---

## Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity focuses specifically on protecting digital systems and electronic information from cyber threats.

While closely related, cybersecurity is considered a subset of the broader discipline of Information Security.

---

# CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is the foundation of modern information security.

It consists of:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability

---

## Confidentiality

Confidentiality ensures that information is accessible only to authorized individuals.

### Common Controls

- Encryption
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Access Control Lists (ACLs)
- VPNs
- Strong passwords

### Example

Only Human Resources staff can access employee salary records.

---

## Integrity

Integrity ensures that information remains accurate, complete, and unaltered.

### Common Controls

- Hashing
- Digital Signatures
- Checksums
- File Integrity Monitoring (FIM)

### Example

A financial transaction cannot be modified without authorization.

---

## Availability

Availability ensures that authorized users can access systems and data whenever required.

### Common Controls

- Backups
- Redundant systems
- Load balancing
- Disaster Recovery (DR)
- Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS)

### Example

Online banking services remain operational despite hardware failures.

# Information Assets

## Definition

An information asset is anything of value that stores, processes, or transmits information.

### Examples

- Customer databases
- Financial records
- Source code
- Passwords
- Encryption keys
- Cloud resources
- Servers
- Laptops
- Intellectual property

---

# Data Classification

Organizations classify information based on sensitivity.

Typical classifications include:

| Classification | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| Public | Freely available information |
| Internal | Intended for internal organizational use |
| Confidential | Sensitive business information |
| Restricted / Highly Confidential | Critical information requiring the highest level of protection |

Data classification helps determine the appropriate security controls.

# Cybersecurity Domains

Cybersecurity consists of several specialized domains.

## Network Security

Protects network infrastructure and communications.

Examples:

- Firewalls
- IDS/IPS
- VPNs
- Network segmentation

---

## Application Security

Focuses on securing software throughout its lifecycle.

Examples:

- Secure coding
- Code reviews
- Web application testing
- Vulnerability scanning

---

## Endpoint Security

Protects user devices such as:

- Desktops
- Laptops
- Smartphones
- Tablets

Common solutions include antivirus, EDR, and device management.

---

## Cloud Security

Protects cloud infrastructure and cloud-hosted services.

Examples:

- Identity and Access Management (IAM)
- Cloud encryption
- Cloud security posture management

---

## Identity and Access Management (IAM)

Ensures that users have appropriate access to resources.

Examples:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- MFA
- Single Sign-On (SSO)

---

## Security Operations (SOC)

Continuously monitors, detects, investigates, and responds to security incidents.

---

## Digital Forensics

Collects, preserves, analyzes, and presents digital evidence after security incidents.

---

## Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)

Aligns cybersecurity with business objectives, risk management, and regulatory requirements.

---

# Basic Security Terminology

## Asset

Anything valuable that requires protection.

---

## Threat

Anything capable of causing harm.

---

## Vulnerability

A weakness that may be exploited.

---

## Exploit

A method used to take advantage of a vulnerability.

---

## Risk

The potential impact when a threat exploits a vulnerability.

# Cybersecurity Best Practices

Organizations should:

- Enable Multi-Factor Authentication
- Use strong passwords
- Apply security patches regularly
- Encrypt sensitive information
- Maintain regular backups
- Monitor security logs
- Conduct vulnerability assessments
- Perform penetration testing
- Provide employee security awareness training
- Implement the Principle of Least Privilege
